# Landslide Safety Guidance

## High Risk Operations
When a district is assessed as high risk, field teams should intensify monitoring in vulnerable slope zones, inspect drainage blockage, restrict access to unstable hill roads where feasible, and prepare emergency communication with local authorities and response teams.

## Moderate Risk Operations
When a district is assessed as moderate risk, authorities should maintain enhanced monitoring, review recent rainfall accumulation, inspect historically vulnerable slopes, and keep local warning channels ready in case the forecast worsens.

## Low Risk Operations
When a district is assessed as low risk, routine observation should continue. Teams should still watch for sudden rainfall escalation, fresh cracks, slope runoff concentration, and road-cut instability in steep terrain.

## Rainfall Trigger Interpretation
Rapid rainfall accumulation and high antecedent rainfall can saturate soil and weaken slope material. Rainfall-driven landslide conditions are especially concerning when short-duration rainfall is followed by continued wet conditions over multiple days.

## Terrain Trigger Interpretation
Steep slopes, elevated terrain, and cut-slope infrastructure increase landslide susceptibility. Terrain-heavy districts should be monitored closely even when rainfall is not extreme, because instability can persist after earlier saturation.

## Graph and Spillover Interpretation
If neighboring districts are already unstable, regional spillover can indicate broader environmental stress. Spatial hotspot signals should be used as supporting evidence rather than as the only basis for warning decisions.
